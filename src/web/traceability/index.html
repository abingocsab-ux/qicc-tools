Standard operating procedure — material traceability

Metallurgy Material Traceability Procedure

Copper / aluminium rod → Drawing T1·T2·T4 → Multiwire T3 → Stranding/Bunching C1·C2·B2–B5 → Screening/Armouring A3·A4
Doc. MTL-SOP-001 · Rev. 14
Metallurgy Section
Coil weight as weighed (own limit) · Tolerance ±1% (configurable)

1 · Purpose

To guarantee full traceability of every rod lot — copper on T1/T3/T4 and predominantly aluminium on T2 — from the incoming coil to the finished conductor drum, recording material consumption and production per MR at every stage, detecting variances at the moment of entry, and managing savings and over-consumptions in a controlled way. This procedure replaces manual handwritten logbooks: each lot number is entered once and all balances are calculated automatically.

3 · Definitions

Rod lot / coil
One incoming rod coil identified by the supplier lot number, entered at the weight actually weighed at receipt. The registered weight is that coil's own drawing limit. Copper coils feed T1/T3/T4; aluminium coils feed T2 (T2 can also run copper when the plan requires).
MR No.
The production order that consumes material. Every run at every stage is recorded against an MR.
Drawing run (T1 · T2 · T4)
Rod breakdown: takes a weight from one coil and produces drawn wire for an MR. Material rules: T1 and T4 are copper only; T2 defaults to aluminium and may be switched to copper per run. Output length is calculated from output weight × wire diameter × material density (Cu 8.89, Al 2.70 g/cm³) and displayed as g/m; the operator may overwrite the length manually — the manual value is kept until the field is cleared.
Multiwire run (T3)
Fine drawing, copper only: input is drawn wire from a T1/T2/T4 MR (selected by rod lot), output is fine wire under the same MR or a new one — the rod lot is inherited either way. Feeds bunching B3–B5 and screening at A3.
Stranding / bunching run (C1 · C2 · B2–B5)
Consumes drawn wire from the Wire source MR and produces conductor on a drum. The output can be booked under a different MR using the Produce conductor for MR field — one source MR's wire can feed several output MRs by saving one run per output.
Screening / armouring run (A3 · A4)
Applies drawn wire onto the running cable. No conductor is consumed and no output weight/length is recorded. The top MR is a reference only for traceability. The supervisor selects the wire source: screen wire from a T3 MR only, copper armour wire from a T1/T2/T4 MR only. The whole available wire of the source MR is issued; the operator enters only scrap and the leftover on the bobbin. Consumption = issued − leftover; the leftover returns to free stock (traced to its rod lot) for allocation.
Wire stock / material saving
Two flows: (a) positive MR wire balance — booked to drawn-wire stock under the original rod lot; (b) A3/A4 leftover — booked as free stock traceable to the source rod lot. Neither goes back onto the coil.
Wire balance (per MR)
Wire in (drawn + allocated leftover) − T3 input − stranding/bunching input − screen wire consumed − armour wire consumed − stock returned.
Coil "Next process" indicator
A coil is 100% accounted for when every kilogram has either moved on (stranding/bunching + screen/armour wire, traced through allocations back to the rod lot) or been recorded as drawing scrap.
Drum status (stranding/bunching)
Released — conductor immediately available to A3/A4 of its output MR. Hold — planning parks the drum in tab 5, where it can be Released to its own MR or Transferred whole to another MR (the rod lot follows the drum).
Over-consumption
Any input exceeding what is available for the MR or the coil beyond tolerance. Allowed only as a controlled deviation with reason and supervisor approval — including drawing beyond a coil's registered weight when the coil weighed heavier than recorded.
Tolerance
Approved allowance (default ±1%) applied to every balance check. Set in the header by the Metallurgy Section.

4 · Procedure — step by step

  1. Register the coil (tab 1). On receipt, enter date, lot number, coil weight as actually weighed, and supplier/heat. Duplicate lots are rejected. The registered weight becomes that coil's own physical limit throughout the system. Deleting a coil cascades: all drawing, stranding/bunching, screening/armouring and stock records traced to it are removed after confirmation — take a Backup first. The coil register keeps its column headers frozen while scrolling and has a lot search box (lot, supplier, MR, date); the Coil Board has the same lot search, and each coil's lot header stays pinned while scrolling through its consumption rows.
  2. Drawing run (tab 2). Select machine. For T1/T2/T4 select the rod coil issued on the payoff; for T3 select the source rod lot. Enter output MR, wire size and material (T1/T3/T4 are locked to copper; T2 defaults to aluminium and can be switched to copper). Enter output kg, scrap, and setup/run minutes. Output length is auto-calculated from weight × Ø × material density and shown as g/m; overwrite it only if the actual metrage differs. Coil consumption is calculated automatically as output + scrap — the coil is issued whole on the payoff, no re-weighing. Exceeding the coil's remaining balance beyond tolerance opens the over-consumption panel and requires an approved deviation (reason + supervisor). The Drawing log below keeps its column headers visible while scrolling and can be filtered with the search box in the log header (rod lot, MR, drawing lot or machine).
  3. Drawing scrap allocation (tab 2). A run booked to a club of MRs (e.g. 24561/24562/24563/24564/24565) carries one scrap figure for the whole group. The Scrap allocation per MR panel under the drawing form splits it into whole kilograms per MR — every MR gets the floor of its exact share and the leftover kilograms go one each to the largest fractions, so the parts always add back to the booked scrap (7 kg over 4 MRs = 2 + 2 + 2 + 1). While the group is still in production the split is marked provisional and uses equal shares; once the group's pooled wire is fully consumed it is marked final and switches to how much of the club's wire each MR actually took (conductor produced for it, screen/armour wire issued to it, stock allocated to it). Either basis can be pinned per group from the Split basis selector. Press Adjust to set the kilograms by hand — the entered shares must add up to the booked scrap — or Back to auto to return to the automatic split. The second table totals the drawing scrap charged to each individual MR number.
  4. Stranding / bunching run (tab 3). Select machine (C1/C2 stranding, B2–B5 bunching) — machine selection filters the wire sources: B3/B4/B5 are fine bunchers and accept T3 fine wire only; rod-drawn wire (T1/T2/T4) runs on B2, C1 or C2, or is re-drawn on T3 first. Then select the Wire source MR whose drawn wire is being consumed — one run consumes ONE wire Ø: if the source MR holds wire of several diameters (e.g. T1 wire at 2.59 partly re-drawn to 1.80 on T3 under the same MR), a Wire Ø used selector appears and the supervisor must pick which diameter this run consumes; the trace panel shows the estimated balance per Ø. Then select the Produce conductor for MR (leave blank if the output belongs to the same MR). Enter the output kg/m, the conductor resistance measured on the drum (Ω/km at 20 °C), scrap, drum number and times — wire consumption is calculated automatically as output + scrap and deducted from the source MR's drawn wire, traced back to the rod lot. Save one run per output MR to split one source across several MRs. Set drum status: Released (available to A3/A4) or Hold (parked in tab 5 until Release or Transfer). Any positive wire balance remaining in the source MR can be moved to stock (tab 5).
  5. Screening / armouring run (tab 4). Select machine A3/A4 and a reference MR (traceability only — the conductor is not consumed here and there is no output weight or length). The supervisor selects the wire source MR (screen wire from T3 only; copper armour wire from T1/T2/T4 only — the rule is checked on the drawing record, so an MR holding both classes offers each class only its own kilograms). One run issues ONE wire Ø: where the source MR holds several diameters — e.g. 24561/24562/24563/24564/24565 carries Ø2.59 drawn on T3 and Ø1.80 drawn on T4 — a Wire Ø used selector appears and only the selected diameter's balance is issued, never the pooled figure. The operator enters only scrap and the leftover on the bobbin; consumption = issued − leftover, and the leftover returns to free stock at that same Ø (traced to its rod lot), allocatable to any MR in tab 5.
  6. MR Detail (tab 6+). The full traceability picture for one MR number at a time. Search or pick the MR — clubbed bookings are broken apart, so selecting 24584 shows only 24584's work and never the sibling MRs it was drawn with. The tab opens with the rod coil lots feeding the MR and the kilograms from each, then every drawing run with operator, rod lot and kg used, draw lot, Ø, consumed and output kg and metres, yield %, run scrap and scrap %, whether the run was booked to a club, and setup/run minutes — with a totals line. A separate table shows the drawing scrap actually charged to this MR and how it was split. Below that: the stranding/bunching runs whose conductor was produced for this MR, screening/armouring, stock movements, and manual scrap adjustments for scrap that never reached a run record (enter kilograms to add, or a minus figure to take scrap back off). Press Print this MR for a one-MR sheet — sections with no records are left off the page entirely. Coil allocation can be overridden here with Edit allocation.
  7. Wire stock management (tab 5). Pick the Month (defaults to the current month) or tick All months for the live to-date view. The KPI strip switches with the filter: All months shows drawn to date, stock booked, allocated, free stock and drums on hold; a chosen month adds Still avail. from month draws — wire drawn in that month that is still on the floor today, attributed FIFO from each MR's oldest consumption. Every table on the tab respects the same month scope. Three flows feed the stock: (a) MRs with a positive wire balance — route it in one step with Allocate to MR (books the stock record under its rod lot and hands it straight to the target MR) or Return to source (sends it back to the MR whose wire this one was re-drawn from, shown only when that parent exists), or park it with Move to stock for later allocation; each movement carries ONE Ø and the Ø column shows the live balance per diameter, so a diameter fully consumed by a re-draw is no longer offered; (b) A3/A4 wire leftovers, booked automatically as free stock traceable to the source rod lot; and separately, conductor drums on hold (stranding/bunching output saved with Hold status) are listed at the top of the tab — press Release to make the drum available to its own MR's screening/armouring, or Transfer to reassign the whole drum to another MR. Press Allocate to MR on free stock to release it to an MR; enter a smaller quantity to split one stock record across several MRs. The per-size chips show free stock by diameter. Press Print wire stock report for a month-scoped PDF (Doc. MTL-WSR-001) for Planning. Un-allocation is blocked once the receiving MR has consumed wire.
  8. Wire by Ø (tab 5+). Planning view that sums every wire diameter: drawn, still available on MR balances, and unallocated free stock. Same Month / All months filter as tab 5: All months totals drawn to date with live MR availability; a chosen month limits drawn kg to that month and uses the same still-available FIFO attribution for MR balances (free stock counts records booked in the month). Read the KPI cards, bar chart and table, then Print for the monthly planning sheet (Doc. MTL-DIA-001).
  9. Correction of entry errors. Use the Edit button on any log row; the record loads into the form, correct it and press Update record. Quantities may be corrected freely within the same physical limits; the MR of a record cannot change once downstream records exist.
  10. Over-consumption (see §6).
  11. Consumption report (tab 7). At end of shift open the Consumption Report, check the date, press Print (or Save as PDF) and send to Planning. Drawing production is grouped by rod lot with per-lot subtotals; the tonnage totals T1·T2·T4 only, and T3 multiwire is shown separately for traceability. Stranding/bunching is also grouped by rod lot with per-lot subtotals, showing Produced for MR ← from Source MR where applicable. The MR filter beside the date restricts the whole report — and therefore the printout — to one MR: the list offers individual MR numbers, and matching covers clubbed MRs (selecting 24552 also catches runs booked as 24551/24552) and drums produced for that MR by transfer. Combine it with All dates to print one MR's complete history from rod lot to armouring. Wide tables can be panned with the frozen scrollbar at the top of each table — printing always shows the full width. Take a data Backup (sidebar button) — the Save-As dialog lets you save the JSON beside the app file so backups and app stay together; the browser then remembers that folder.
  12. Copper consumption vs 2025 — SMP saving (tab 7+). A standalone monthly review that reads stranding/bunching output only and never touches the traceability balances. For each conductor size it compares the period's Cu wt. average (kg/km) — the mean of each run's output kg ÷ output km — against the frozen 2025 average for that size (taken from the "2025 Avg" sheet of the Metallurgy Resistance & Weight workbook). Saving (kg) = (2025 avg − actual) × length when positive; Lost (kg) when the actual exceeds the 2025 average; K€ = kg × the Cu €/kg value in the header (default 5.5 €/kg, editable). Pick the month (or All months), read the KPI cards and the green/red bar chart per size, and Print for the monthly review. Each size also shows the average measured resistance against the standard max (workbook value × CR factor): because resistance is inverse to copper weight, R near the max with weight below the 2025 average = saving verified; R clearly below the max confirms extra copper where the weight shows over-consumption; and R above the standard max is non-conforming regardless of the weight result — check the run and the measurement. A size badged "no 2025 baseline" means the conductor size text doesn't match the 2025 table — correct the size spelling on the run entry. Baselines are embedded constants: when the 2025 reference table is revised, the app copy must be updated with it.
  13. Production daily report (tab 9 — Form PRO012F). The operator logs the shift on screen exactly as on the paper form: header (date, machine, shift, operator, supervisor), then one entry per time slot with in/out times (minutes calculated automatically, the next slot's start pre-fills from the last slot's end), loss-time code, MR, lot number and drum no., type/size, length, scrap and remarks. Times are entered by tapping, never by scrolling or typing: tap the field, then either ⏱ Now (stamps the current clock), = last out (chains from the previous slot's end for the same date and machine), or hour → tens of minutes → exact minute for any time to 1-minute precision — a 2-minute stop is two taps on the minutes. On save, lot, MR and type/size stay for the next slot; the drum clears. Utilization % and the top loss code are computed live, and the report prints in the PRO012F layout. Division of records — no double entry of output: PRO012F is the time record (slots, loss codes, per-slot length, shift scrap); the run entries on tabs 2–4 are the material record. Output weight exists only on the run entry (it drives the copper balance; length there is auto-calculated from weight × Ø × density), and leave setup/run minutes blank on tab 2 and tab 3 — they are taken automatically from this report (run = Σ code O, setup = Σ D1+D3 for the same date, machine and MR). A3/A4 does not record setup/run times. Scrap kg on PRO012F is the operator's shift record only; the scrap that counts for the material balance belongs on the run entry.
  14. Monthly inventory (tab 8). At month close, select the month and print (Doc. MTL-INV-001). It shows the consumption history per lot — opening, received, consumed and remaining — plus the month's production and scrap totals, with Drawing rod breakdown and T3 multiwire as separate balanced rows so no kilogram is buried. Reconcile the closing figures against the physical count with Stores; any difference is investigated as unrecorded scrap before sign-off.

5 · Verification rules

R1 — Drawing run: Coil consumption = Output + Scrap (auto — coil issued whole on payoff)
R2 — Coil: Coil weight (as registered) = Σ takes by all MRs + Remaining on coil (over-draw beyond tolerance → controlled deviation)
R3 — Drawing length: Output length (m) = Output weight ÷ (π/4·Ø²·ρ) — auto, editable · Cu 8.89, Al 2.70 g/cm³
R4 — Machine material: T1/T3/T4 = Copper only · T2 default Aluminium (switchable to Copper per run)
R5 — MR wire balance: T3 input + Strand/Bunch input + Screen wire + Armour wire + Stock out ≤ Drawn wire + Allocated leftover (± tol)
R6 — Strand/Bunch run: Wire consumption = Output + Scrap (auto), deducted from the Wire source MR; output belongs to the Produce-for MR (may differ from the source)
R7 — Screening/armouring: NO conductor is consumed at A3/A4 · no output weight/length recorded · reference MR is traceability only
R8 — A3/A4 wire sources: Screen wire = T3 fine wire only · Copper armour wire = T1/T2/T4 drawn wire only · tested on the drawing record, not on the MR
R8b — One A3/A4 run issues ONE wire Ø: if the source MR holds several diameters the Ø must be selected, only that Ø's balance is issued, and the leftover returns to stock at that same Ø
R8c — Every wire-stock movement carries ONE Ø · a balance may go to another MR or back to the parent MR it was re-drawn from, never back to itself (that movement cancels out) and never back to the rod coil
R9 — A3/A4 wire consumption: Consumed = Issued − Leftover · leftover returns to free stock (rod-lot traced) — allocatable to any MR
R10 — Drum hold: a stranded/bunched drum saved with HOLD keeps its reference MR but cannot be consumed by A3/A4 until planning Releases it (same MR) or Transfers it whole to another MR — the rod lot follows the drum
R11 — Once drawn wire is consumed by stranding/bunching it becomes a finished conductor: it never reappears as drawn-wire stock and cannot be selected for screening/armouring
R12 — Conductor resistance (Ω/km, 20 °C) ≤ standard max per size (workbook value × CR factor) · R is inverse to Cu weight: high R near the max ⇒ lighter conductor (saving) · low R ⇒ extra copper (over-consumption)
R13 — Buncher wire source: B3/B4/B5 = T3 fine wire ONLY · rod-drawn wire (T1/T2/T4) is bunched/stranded on B2 · C1 · C2 or re-drawn on T3 first

6 · Over-consumption management (controlled deviation)

First check: is it really over-consumption? Often the MR simply lacks allocated wire. The deviation panel now offers to fix that directly: allocate free stock to the MR with one tap (only the shortfall is taken — the rest stays free in tab 5), or jump to tab 2 with the MR pre-filled to draw additional rod. Only when the excess is real does the deviation apply:

  1. Prevent first: if a run needs more material than is available, the preferred actions are ① draw additional rod for the MR (tab 2) or ② allocate leftover stock (tab 5).
  2. Deviate under control: if the excess is real (extra scrap, rework, measurement variance, plan shortage, or a coil that weighed heavier than registered), the system requires a reason and supervisor approval before the run can be saved. This applies to drawing (coil and T3), stranding/bunching, and A3/A4 wire consumption. Registered physical limits become an approved deviation, never a silent override.
  3. Register and report: every approved deviation is badged in the logs, listed in the Over-consumption register (tab 6) and printed in Section F of the daily report for Planning and the Production Supervisor.
Example — over-consumption: MR240002 has 1,180 kg wire available; the bunching run on B4 needs 1,205 kg. The operator first checks tab 5 for wire stock. None fits, and the excess is due to re-threading scrap, so he enters reason "re-threading scrap after wire break" and approver "Supervisor Ali". The run saves, flagged Over 25 kg, and appears in the register and in Section F of that day's report.

7 · Worked example — one coil, several MRs

① Coil LOT123 registered on T1 payoff at 4,120 kg as weighed — that weight becomes the coil's own limit.
② T1 draws 1,500 kg of LOT123 for MR240001 (Ø1.80, Cu): out 1,480 kg, scrap 20 kg. Length auto = 1,480 ÷ (π/4·1.80²·8.89/1000) ≈ 65,442 m.
③ T2 draws 2,000 kg of LOT123 for MR240002 (Ø2.20, Cu — operator switched T2 from default Al): out 1,975 kg, scrap 25 kg. Coil remaining: 620 kg.
④ T3 multiwire (Cu) takes 800 kg of MR240002 wire → fine wire under MR240010 (Ø0.40, Cu): out 790 kg — rod lot LOT123 is inherited.
⑤ C1 strands with Wire source MR240001 and Produce conductor for MR240001: out 1,380 kg to drum DR-05, scrap 20 kg → consumption 1,400 kg; MR balance +80 kg → moved to stock under LOT123 (tab 5).
⑥ B4 bunches with Wire source MR240002, output split: run A produces 700 kg for MR240002 (drum DR-08), run B produces 460 kg for MR240007 (drum DR-09) — same source, two output MRs.
⑦ The 80 kg drawn-wire stock is allocated to MR240007 and consumed on the next bunch; its record shows source lot LOT123.
⑧ A3 runs on reference MR240001: screen wire from MR240010 issued whole (790 kg), 30 kg leftover on bobbin → 760 kg consumed, 30 kg returns to free stock (LOT123). No conductor and no output weight/length are recorded at A3.
⑨ Board answer for LOT123: 1,500 kg → MR240001 · 2,000 kg → MR240002 (800 kg re-drawn on T3 for MR240010) · 80 kg saved → MR240007 · 30 kg returned as free stock · 620 kg remaining on coil. Next-process = 100% when every kilogram has either moved on or been recorded as scrap.

8 · Records and responsibilities

  • Operators (T, C, B, A machines): enter every run at start/end of production, including times, scrap and leftovers.
  • Shift supervisor: approves over-consumptions; takes the daily data Backup; first review of variance flags.
  • Metallurgy Section: lot verification, leftover allocation, disposition of Investigate/Mismatch flags, monthly CSV export, and the monthly Cu consumption vs 2025 (SMP saving) review including upkeep of the 2025 baseline table, plus the Speed/OEE review and upkeep of the editable line-speed master.
  • Production Supervisor: reviews and approves the daily report and monthly records.

9 · Multi-user operation & data integrity

In the cloud build the app is synchronised live through the team cloud backend (this standalone file stores data only in your browser). Cloud version: every record is stored individually, and a save writes only the records that changed — several operators entering runs at the same time never overwrite each other's work, and each new entry appears on colleagues' screens within seconds ("● live — synced with team" in the sidebar). Three rules keep the shared data clean:

  1. One editor per record. If two people edit the same record simultaneously, the last save wins without warning — corrections to a run belong to the machine's operator or one nominated supervisor.
  2. Availability checks are per screen. Two operators drawing from the same nearly-empty coil within the same few seconds can both pass the check and jointly over-draw it; the system does not lose either record — the coil goes negative and is flagged immediately. Treat such a flag like any variance: investigate and correct or register a deviation (§6).
  3. Watch the save confirmation. A failed save shows "Save failed — check your connection" and is retried on the next action; do not close the browser after a failed save until it has gone through. Viewer accounts can browse and print but their changes are never saved.
